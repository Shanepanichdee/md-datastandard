# Data Standard Governance Practices

## Executive Summary
Establishing and governing data standards requires a structured operating model, clearly defined roles such as data stewards, and targeted change management. A successful governance program avoids overly complex bureaucracies, relying instead on "right-sized" decision-making forums, operationalized stewardship, and data literacy initiatives to drive adoption.

## 1. Governance Operating Models
An effective data governance operating model translates policies into execution by defining decision rights, accountability, and escalation paths [1].
- **Decision Architecture over Structure**: Governance structures should follow decision architecture. Programs should focus on defining who owns specific decisions (e.g., definition, ownership, access, quality) rather than creating large, ineffective enterprise councils [2].
- **Operating Archetypes**: Organizations must choose an operating model that fits their culture and size. Small or highly regulated entities may prefer **Centralized Governance**, whereas large or complex organizations often succeed with **Federated or Decentralized Governance**, where central teams provide standards but business domains own the execution [1].

## 2. Role of Data Stewards
Data stewardship is the operational layer that transforms governance theory into practice [3].
- **Enforcement and Quality**: Stewards are responsible for ensuring data quality, enforcing standards, and managing specific data domains [3].
- **Domain-Centric Ownership**: The best practice is to assign stewardship by data domains (e.g., master data, reference data) so that individuals closest to the data are accountable for its quality and consistency [1].

## 3. Change Management for Adoption
The failure of data governance programs is often linked to poor change management rather than flawed operating models [4].
- **Audience-Specific Strategies**: Organizations must tailor their change management approaches to different audiences, recognizing that an executive sponsor, a data owner, and a data steward have different motivations and requirements [4].
- **Aligning Technology and Literacy**: Successful adoption, as seen in Florida Health's data modernization initiative, aligns technological rollouts with robust data literacy programming and cultural transformation [5].
