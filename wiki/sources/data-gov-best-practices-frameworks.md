## Summary
Traditional data governance rests on four pillars (Quality, Stewardship, Protection, Management) operationalized by DAMA-DMBOK's 11 knowledge areas and DCAM's 1–6 maturity scale. Best practice is phased implementation — prioritize 2–3 high-impact areas first, establish standardized terminology, assign clear accountability, and tie all goals to measurable business outcomes.

## Key Claims
- **Four foundational pillars**: Data Quality, Data Stewardship, Data Protection & Compliance, Data Management & Architecture.
- **DAMA-DMBOK**: 11 knowledge areas in the "DAMA Wheel" with Data Governance at the center. Phased rollout required — start with highest-impact gap (often Data Quality or Metadata Management).
- **DCAM maturity scale**: 1 (Not initiated) → 6 (Enhanced). Three component groups: Foundation (Strategy & Funding), Execution (Architecture, Quality, Governance), Collaboration (Data Control Environment).
- **Key Data Elements (KDEs)** and shared semantic vocabulary are prerequisites for cross-team collaboration.
- Assigning data owners, stewards, and custodians early removes ambiguity and prevents escalation loops.
- Governance goals must tie to measurable business outcomes — not function as IT administrative checklists.
- Track Key Quality Indicators (KQIs) and KPIs regularly against DCAM benchmarks.
