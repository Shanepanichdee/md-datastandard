## Summary
Governance programs fail due to flawed operating models, not technology. Successful implementations distribute accountability to business domains, secure non-IT executive sponsorship, solve specific pain points within 90 days, and embed governance as "invisible friction" in existing workflows. Clear decision rights and defined escalation paths ("runways") are prerequisites — roles without decision rights are symbolic.

## Key Claims
- **Start with pain, not policy**: Solve one specific, quantifiable business pain point first (e.g., inventory data causing stockouts). Deliver measurable outcome within 90 days to build momentum.
- **Federated/embedded model**: Business domains own governance outcomes; central team provides tools, standards, coordination. Must align with org's existing culture and decision-making DNA.
- **Business sponsorship required**: Without a visible business sponsor (CFO, COO, CDO) — not IT — middle management will bypass governance processes.
- **Decision rights specificity**: CDO = strategy; Domain Data Owners = accountability for quality/usage; Business Data Stewards = day-to-day execution. Governance only owns decisions requiring enterprise consistency or risk control — everything else stays with operations.
- **Governance "runway"**: Defined intake → triage → design authority → exception handling path prevents ad-hoc escalations and makes governance a predictable service.
- **Invisible friction**: Governance checks embedded in sprint reviews, pipelines, and daily tools — not separate meetings or quarterly audits.
- **Anti-pattern — Governance-by-committee**: Large cross-functional councils lead to paralysis, slow decisions, watered-down policies.
- **Anti-pattern — Tool-first thinking**: Buying a data catalog before defining operating model turns it into a "museum of stale data."
- **Anti-pattern — Maturity model trap**: Advancing maturity levels measures activity, not business impact.
- **Anti-pattern — IT ownership**: Treating governance as an IT initiative guarantees business treats it as optional.
- **Gap**: Limited concrete guidance on HR compensation/bonus structures to incentivize stewardship. Post-implementation decay (years 3–5) under-researched.
