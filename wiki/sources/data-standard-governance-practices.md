## Summary
Successful data standards governance requires right-sized operating models (federated for large orgs, centralized for small/regulated), domain-centric data stewardship, and audience-specific change management. Failure is almost always change management failure, not technical. Florida Health's modernization success is cited as evidence that aligning tech rollout with data literacy programs is critical.

## Key Claims
- Governance operating models must follow "decision architecture" — focus on who owns which decisions, not org chart structure.
- Centralized governance fits small or highly regulated orgs; federated/decentralized fits large or complex enterprises.
- Data stewards are the "operational layer" — enforce quality, manage domains, resolve issues day-to-day.
- Domain-centric stewardship: assign stewards by data domain (master data, reference data) so accountability sits with those closest to the data.
- Change management must be tailored per audience: executives, data owners, and stewards have different motivations and requirements.
- Technology rollout must be paired with data literacy programs — cultural transformation is as important as the tools.
- Florida Health case study: data modernization success attributed to aligning tech deployment with comprehensive literacy programming.
