01 · Overview

What This Research Covers

§

The Core Problem

Organizations accumulate data faster than they can govern it. Without standardized definitions, structural contracts, and enforcement mechanisms, data becomes a liability — siloed, contradictory, and untrustworthy at the moment of decision.

→

The Research Lens

This compendium synthesizes practitioner frameworks (DAMA-DMBOK, DCAM), emerging patterns (data mesh, data contracts), and technical enforcement tools (Great Expectations, Unity Catalog, Databricks DLT) into a unified taxonomy for enterprise data governance.

Mature Frameworks

DAMA-DMBOK and DCAM provide decades of institutional knowledge. DCAM's 9-capability model offers the most operationalizable enterprise governance architecture available.

Emerging Patterns

Data contracts (ODCS format) are shifting governance left. Federated ownership via data mesh resolves the central-vs-distributed tension without sacrificing quality guarantees.

Technical Stack

Great Expectations, Unity Catalog, Databricks DLT, and Atlan represent a converging toolchain for automated, metadata-driven enforcement at pipeline and catalog layers.

03 · Frameworks

Governance Landscape

Key frameworks, architectures, and organizational patterns synthesized across all nine source documents.

DAMA-DMBOK

Framework

11-domain knowledge body covering data architecture, MDM, data quality, security, and warehousing. The canonical reference for enterprise data management roles and processes.

5 sources

DCAM

Framework

EDM Council's 9-capability maturity model: data management strategy, governance, architecture, quality, MDM, platform, operations, usage, and culture. Most operationalizable enterprise framework.

2 sources

Data Mesh

Architecture

Federated data ownership with domain-level accountability, data-as-products, self-serve infrastructure, and federated computational governance. Resolves centralization vs. agility tension.

2 sources

Federated Governance

Pattern

Distributed governance model where central teams set standards and platforms while domain teams own data quality. Enables scale without single-team bottlenecks.

5 sources

Data Stewardship

Role / Process

Human-layer accountability covering business stewards (semantic accuracy), technical stewards (structural integrity), and operational stewards (pipeline health and SLA adherence).

5 sources

AI Governance

Emerging

Extends classical governance to ML/AI pipelines: training data lineage, bias detection, model drift monitoring, feature store contracts, and MLOps lifecycle standards.

2 sources
Anti-patterns
Governance Theater

Policies documented but not enforced. Committees that produce artifacts with no downstream impact.

Shadow Governance

Unofficial data sharing outside sanctioned channels. Bypasses standards invisibly until a breach.

Tool-First Thinking

Buying a catalog tool before defining ownership, stewardship, and quality SLAs.

Standards Without Automation

Manual compliance checks that cannot scale beyond a 3-person team or a quarterly audit cycle.

05 · Toolchain

The Modern Stack

Tools that operationalize governance. Each addresses a specific enforcement layer in the pipeline.

Great Expectations

Quality

Python-native DQ framework. Expectation suites encode quality rules as code. Generates human-readable docs and CI gate reports.

Unity Catalog

Catalog / Security

Databricks unified governance layer. Fine-grained ABAC for tables, views, volumes. Automated PII tagging and lineage tracking.

Databricks DLT

Pipeline

Delta Live Tables — declarative pipelines with built-in expectations. Automates Medallion architecture with lineage and quality metrics.

Atlan

Data Catalog

Modern active metadata platform. Surfaces business context, lineage, and quality scores to consumers. Connects governance to data discovery.

ODCS

Contract Standard

Open Data Contract Standard. YAML-based schema for declaring structural, quality, SLA, and ownership requirements between producers and consumers.

Snowflake Horizon

Governance Suite

Snowflake's unified governance suite covering classification, masking policies, row-level security, and cross-cloud data sharing with governance.

CI/CD Enforcement Pattern

The convergence point: data contracts validated in PR pipelines, schema changes blocked if they break downstream consumer expectations, quality expectations run as pre-merge gates, and catalog metadata updated automatically on merge. Standards become the branching strategy.

06 · Open Questions

What Remains Unsolved

Gaps and tensions surfaced across the research that do not yet have clear practitioner consensus.

?

How do we version-manage data standards across distributed domain teams?

Git-native contract versioning exists, but integration with org-wide policy enforcement is immature. Most teams still rely on manual review.

?

Can AI-generated metadata be trusted for governance decisions?

LLM-assisted tagging (PII detection, business term suggestion) is gaining adoption, but explainability and audit requirements remain unresolved.

?

What is the minimum viable governance tier for a startup vs. enterprise?

Frameworks like DAMA-DMBOK and DCAM were designed for large enterprises. Lightweight operationalizations for <50-person data teams are not well-documented.

?

How does data mesh governance work when domains span multiple cloud providers?

Cross-cloud lineage, federated policy enforcement, and consistent RBAC across AWS + Azure + GCP remain partially solved.
